Business Intelligence

The backbone of decision making.

Why do you need BI?

The academia calls it the »VUCA-world«, being the volatile, uncertain, complex, and ambiguous work environment that can be found everywhere these days. Taking decisions in such environments is equally tough as it is required and strongly depends on data. It’s impossible to be right all the time but with the right foundation it is achievable to take correct decision frequently.

Decision-making can be broken down in five parts.

  • Formulating the problem. That done, you already know there is an issue which is great.
  • Identify the goals to address the issue
  • Find the available options that support the goals
  • Pick one of the alternatives
  • Make further decisions during implementation

Eventually the decisions enterprises must make are predicated upon the presence of viable options to choose from. A mix of tools, programming languages and analytical skills are what it takes to consolidate information into options.

Supporting my client’s decision-making process by providing relevant business intelligence is part of my daily routine. I will be happy to provide these services to you!

Software Strategy

Choosing the right tech stack.

Why do you need software strategy?

As in general company strategy, software strategy can be broken down into several segments with each one equally contributing to its success. The most relevant are:

  • Which tech stack to use?
  • Make or buy decision making!
  • Sourcing of personnel!

The tech stack chosen has the biggest impact on the other segments as compatibility of technology will determine what other technology can be bought or licensed from 3rd parties. Moreover, each technology has its level of popularity and thus a core of support, or lack thereof, which will affect recruitment of qualified personnel.

With over 15 years in enterprise grade software development and IT infrastructure I am providing guidance to my customers in selecting frontend framework solutions, application layer technology and scalable databases, with a proclivity towards Linux based infrastructure. That being said, I am proficient in utilizing the Microsoft toolchain and have access to engineers and experts on either side.

As a rule of thumb, not many technology-stacks can be scaled ad infinitum. Reviewing project scope, business plan and company strategy is paramount to my work in suggesting and/or implementing the tech-stack and 3rd party software eventually being deployed.

With 10+ years in collaborating with recruiting agencies and nearshoring partners, conducting hundreds of interviews from junior to senior staff and an average staff retention rate of 5+ years, I know where to find the right people for the job & suitable to the projects budget.

Tactical Management

Beyond strategic planning.

Why do you need tactical management?

Strategy is suggesting what you are supposed to do, tactical management is working out how you should do it. It’s the eternal sequence of first »doing the rights things« and then »doing those things the right way«!

Tactical management will use alternating management methodology conducive to achieving the objectives that are supporting long-term strategy by navigating day-to-day operations through pertinent initiatives. Tasks and responsibilities attributable to tactical managers include:

  • Conflict management and dispute resolution
  • Knowledge management and documentation
  • Sourcing and training of personnel
  • Risk management
  • Reporting and data analysis
  • Acting as communication interface

Day-to-day operations is following ongoing topics, often chasing short term goals and KPIs. With the view narrowed down on these near-term matters, it will become difficult to see how a transition purported in long-term strategy may materialize under present circumstances. For this reason, tactical managers are cornerstone assets as change agents in transforming a status-quo-mission into a 5-years-vision, thus bridging the gap between strategists and operations.

Weather teams are organized under agile- or traditional project-management-principles, productive workspaces must be well structured and intuitive, providing low-level information alongside with visual reference towards its bigger picture.

Creating structure, driving change, and navigating teams towards delivering on objectives has been my domain for the past 15+ years, using suitable management methodology and cutting-edge toolchains.
